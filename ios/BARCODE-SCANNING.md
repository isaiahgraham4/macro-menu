# Product barcode scanning

Entry points: Today → Scan product barcode, My foods → Scan product barcode, and the Food details editor. The camera uses VisionKit on supported devices for EAN-13 (including UPC-A), EAN-8 and ITF-14. Manual entry accepts checksum-valid GTIN-8/12/13/14. The scanner stops after its first valid match and when dismissed. Denied camera permission and unsupported devices retain manual entry.

Lookups use the public Open Food Facts v2 product endpoint with a custom User-Agent and a 20-second timeout. Only barcode numbers are sent; camera frames are processed locally. API documentation: https://openfoodfacts.github.io/openfoodfacts-server/api/ . Product source links and ODbL attribution appear in the review screen and saved food notes. Review data against the package; this is a community database, not a guarantee of product coverage or accuracy.

The parser prefers complete per-serving nutrition with an explicit serving description. Otherwise, it converts a complete per-100 g/mL basis using the serving's stated weight or volume. If that amount is missing, the editor requires a serving amount before saving. It never mixes the two bases. Explicit kcal takes priority; kJ is divided by 4.184 when kcal is absent. Normalized sodium is converted from grams to milligrams. Unknown optional nutrients remain absent. Products missing energy or protein fall back to nutrition-label scanning/manual entry instead of inventing zeroes. Prepared-product nutrition isn't substituted for as-sold nutrition.

Selecting a match fills the existing editor for review and serving selection; it never saves or logs automatically. Today saves the reviewed portion to the selected day's log, while My foods saves it to the user's foods. Network errors, missing products and incomplete products provide retry/manual paths. Changing or dismissing the lookup cancels its task.

Barcodes on your own foods: every food has an optional barcode (Food details → Barcode, typed or read with the camera button, which captures digits without a lookup). Scanning checks the user's saved foods before Open Food Facts, ignoring leading zeros so UPC-A and its EAN-13 form match, and shows the match under "In your foods". When Open Food Facts has no product or incomplete data, the scanner offers "Add barcode to a new food", which opens the editor with the barcode filled in. Products filled from Open Food Facts keep their barcode too, so a saved copy is found offline next time. The editor warns when another saved food already has the same barcode, and blocks saving a barcode that fails its checksum. My foods search also matches barcode digits.

Today offers a number-of-servings field and a total preview, while preserving the food's nutrition for one serving. Label OCR also prefers the column headed per serving (in either column order), detects labelled serving weights/volumes, and converts per-100-only values when possible. Unrecognised column headings require the user to confirm the basis. Saved foods retain their one-serving definition and offer portion quantity through Food details.

Checks: `bash ios/Tests/run-checks.sh` includes barcode checksums, kcal/kJ priority, numeric-string values, serving basis isolation, missing fields, sodium conversion and matching saved foods by barcode. Live camera recognition must also be exercised on a physical device with real packaging.
